Aftercare

Your massage does not end when you leave the table. These simple, realistic habits can help you stay comfortable, support recovery, and care for your body during the next 24 to 48 hours.

Quick aftercare checklist

Start with the basics

  • Drink water according to your normal needs.
  • Choose gentle movement over complete inactivity.
  • Eat normally and prioritize a nourishing meal.
  • Give yourself time to rest if you feel tired.
  • Notice how your body responds without overanalyzing it.
  • Contact FlowTherapy if something concerns you.

Your Aftercare Timeline

You do not need a complicated routine. Focus on a few useful actions at the times they are most likely to fit naturally into your day.

1

Immediately After

  • Stand up slowly and notice how you feel.
  • Drink water if you are thirsty.
  • Avoid rushing straight into intense physical activity.
  • Give yourself a few minutes to transition back into your day.
  • Be cautious if you feel especially relaxed, sleepy, or lightheaded.
2

Later Today

  • Take a comfortable walk or move gently.
  • Eat a normal, nourishing meal.
  • Change positions instead of sitting for long periods.
  • Use comfortable warmth if it helps you relax.
  • Prioritize a calm evening and quality sleep.
3

Next 24–48 Hours

  • Notice whether movement feels easier or different.
  • Continue gentle activity as tolerated.
  • Modify strenuous exercise if you feel unusually sore.
  • Pay attention to discomfort that is persistent or worsening.
  • Follow any specific guidance discussed during your session.

Simple Habits That Support Recovery

Aftercare should support your life—not become another demanding wellness checklist.

Helpful choices

  • Drink water based on thirst and your usual hydration needs.
  • Walk, stretch gently, or change positions throughout the day.
  • Allow yourself extra rest if your body feels tired.
  • Choose a comfortable shower, bath, or heating pad when appropriate.
  • Eat normally and avoid skipping meals.
  • Take note of any helpful changes you want to mention next time.

Avoid if possible

  • Testing your limits immediately after a deep or focused session.
  • Forcing aggressive stretches into sore or sensitive tissue.
  • Assuming you must drink excessive amounts of water.
  • Ignoring sharp, severe, unusual, or worsening pain.
  • Using intense heat or ice without protecting your skin.
  • Expecting every massage session to produce the same response.

What You Might Notice

Everyone responds differently. These experiences may occur, but no particular response is required for a massage to have been useful.

Feeling relaxed or sleepy

A quieter evening or earlier bedtime may feel especially appealing after your session.

Temporary tenderness

Mild soreness may occur after focused work, particularly in areas that were already sensitive or overworked.

Easier movement

You may notice that turning, reaching, walking, or changing positions feels more comfortable.

Emotional shifts

Some people feel calm, reflective, energized, tired, or simply unchanged after a massage.

Increased body awareness

You may become more aware of posture, tension, breathing, or areas that need additional support.

Gradual changes

Some benefits are noticed immediately, while others become clearer after movement, sleep, or repeated care.

Common Aftercare Questions

Open any question for a quick answer without leaving this page.

How much water should I drink after a massage?

Drink according to your normal needs and thirst. Massage does not require forcing excessive amounts of water. Hydration needs vary based on body size, activity, climate, medications, and health conditions.

Is soreness after massage normal?

Mild, temporary tenderness can occur after focused or deeper work. It should generally improve rather than become progressively worse. Severe, sharp, persistent, or concerning pain deserves professional evaluation.

Should I use heat or ice?

Heat may feel soothing for general tightness or stiffness, while cold may feel more comfortable after a fresh irritation or swelling. Protect your skin, limit exposure, and avoid either option when it is medically inappropriate for you.

Can I exercise after my massage?

Gentle activity is often reasonable when you feel well. Strenuous exercise immediately after an intensive session may not feel comfortable for everyone. Adjust the intensity based on how your body responds and the type of work you received.

Should I stretch after my session?

Gentle, comfortable stretching may feel helpful. Avoid forcing range of motion or stretching aggressively into pain. Walking and ordinary movement may be enough.

What if I feel tired afterward?

Feeling relaxed or sleepy can happen. Give yourself time to transition, eat and hydrate normally, and rest when possible. Seek medical care for severe weakness, fainting, confusion, or other concerning symptoms.

When aftercare should include medical care

Massage aftercare is not a substitute for medical evaluation. Seek prompt care for symptoms such as:

  • Severe, sudden, or rapidly worsening pain
  • Chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or confusion
  • New weakness, numbness, loss of coordination, or difficulty speaking
  • Significant swelling, redness, warmth, or signs of infection
  • Symptoms following a serious injury
  • Any reaction that feels urgent or unsafe

Your recovery matters

Every body responds differently. If you have a question about how you feel after your session, please reach out rather than wondering whether your response is normal.
